\section{Conventions and final geometry}

The structure is invariant along $z$, periodic along $x$, and occupies the
half-space $y\leq0$.  Water fills $y>0$ and the two grooves; every solid
boundary is treated as acoustically rigid.  The convention is
$p\propto\e^{+\ii\omega t}$.  Outgoing propagating waves vary as
$\e^{-\ii k_y y}$, while evanescent waves have $\operatorname{Im}k_y<0$.

The final design was obtained at the exact $n=-1$ Rayleigh relation
$\Omega=1-\kappa$.  Its normalized and dimensional parameters are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:geometry}.  The dimensional realization uses
$c=1500~\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $f_{\BIC}=180~\mathrm{kHz}$.

The Bloch point is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \kappa_{\BIC}&=0.110000105311808,\\
 \Omega_{\BIC}&=0.889999894688192,\\
 \theta_{\BIC}&=7.0996629128^{\circ}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:s-point}
\end{equation}
At this point $n=0$ is propagating, $n=-1$ is exactly grazing, and $n=+1$
is evanescent.  Negative $\kappa$ gives the reciprocal partner and exchanges
$n=-1$ with $n=+1$.

\section{Full modal-matching operator}

The exterior field of a homogeneous mode is
\begin{equation}
 p^{\homg}(x,y)=\sum_{n=-M}^{M}A_n
 \e^{-\ii k_{x,n}x-\ii k_{y,n}y},
 \quad k_{x,n}=k_x+\frac{2\pi n}{a},
 \label{eq:s-exterior}
\end{equation}
with $N=2M+1$ retained orders.  In groove $\ell$,
\begin{equation}
 p_\ell(x,y)=\sum_{q=0}^{K-1}C_{\ell q}
 \cos\!\left[\frac{q\pi(x-x_\ell)}{w_\ell}\right]
 \cos[\beta_{\ell q}(y+d_\ell)],
 \label{eq:s-groove}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \beta_{\ell q}^2=k_0^2-\left(\frac{q\pi}{w_\ell}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
No single-longitudinal-mode approximation is made.  In particular, the large
groove retains the transverse $q=1$ family that dominates the final state.

Define the aperture projection
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_{n,\ell q}=\frac{1}{w_\ell}
 \int_{x_\ell}^{x_\ell+w_\ell}
 \e^{-\ii k_{x,n}x}
 \cos\!\left[\frac{q\pi(x-x_\ell)}{w_\ell}\right]\mathrm{d}x.
 \label{eq:s-projection}
\end{equation}
Projection of pressure and normal-velocity continuity gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf F
 \begin{bmatrix}\bm A\\\bm C\end{bmatrix}=0,\qquad
 \mathsf F=
 \begin{bmatrix}
  \mathsf B&-\mathsf C_d\\
  \operatorname{diag}(k_{y,n}/k_0)&\ii\mathsf D\mathsf V_d
 \end{bmatrix},
 \label{eq:s-full}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 \mathsf C_d&=\operatorname{diag}[\cos(\beta_{\ell q}d_\ell)],\\
 \mathsf V_d&=\operatorname{diag}[(\beta_{\ell q}/k_0)
 \sin(\beta_{\ell q}d_\ell)].
\end{align}
The groove coefficients are retained as unknowns, avoiding the artificial
tangent poles that arise when cavity admittances are formed before the
homogeneous eigenproblem.

For driven scattering, the incident and reflected amplitudes are separated
from the homogeneous vector.  With
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf S=\mathsf D\operatorname{diag}
 [-\ii\beta_{\ell q}\tan(\beta_{\ell q}d_\ell)],
\end{equation}
the reflected coefficients obey
\begin{equation}
 [\operatorname{diag}(k_{y,n})-\mathsf S\mathsf B]\bm A^{\driv}
 =k_{y,0}\bm e_0+\mathsf S\bm b_{\mathrm{inc}}.
 \label{eq:s-driven}
\end{equation}
For every propagating order,
\begin{equation}
 \eta_n=\frac{\operatorname{Re}k_{y,n}}{k_{y,0}}
 |A_n^{\driv}|^2,\qquad \sum_{n\in\mathrm{prop}}\eta_n=1
 \label{eq:s-power}
\end{equation}
in the lossless calculation.

\section{Strict Rayleigh criterion and endpoint test}

For $k_{y,n}=0$, a homogeneous order contributes
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^H|p_n|^2\mathrm{d}y=H|A_n|^2.
 \label{eq:s-linear-norm}
\end{equation}
It therefore remains non-square-integrable even though its normal flux
vanishes.  At positive $\kappa_{\BIC}$, strict localization requires
\begin{equation}
 A_{-1}=A_0=0.
 \label{eq:s-strict}
\end{equation}

The constrained calculation removes these two amplitudes from the unknown
vector and evaluates the scaled reduced operator.  For $(N,K)=(345,43)$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{\sigma_{\min}}{\sigma_{\max}}&=4.9107721\times10^{-16},\\
 r_{\mathrm{raw}}&=9.8731869\times10^{-14}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:s-strictres}
\end{equation}
This constraint is not used as the only test.  We independently evaluate the
complete square operator at Eq.~\eqref{eq:s-point}, inserting
$k_{y,-1}=0$ exactly rather than relying on a floating threshold classifier.
The full operator gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{\sigma_{\min}^{\mathrm{full}}}{\sigma_{\max}^{\mathrm{full}}}
 &=5.8469558\times10^{-16},\\
 r_{\mathrm{raw}}^{\mathrm{full}}&=1.2808319\times10^{-13}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:s-fullres}
\end{equation}
The unconstrained endpoint null has
$|A_0|/\|\bm C\|=2.58\times10^{-16}$ and
$|A_{-1}|/\|\bm C\|=8.09\times10^{-17}$.  These are the physically relevant
amplitude zeros; zero threshold power alone is not used as evidence.

\section{Outgoing-pole spectrum and continuation}

Complex eigenfrequencies were obtained with all-channel outgoing/decaying
boundary conditions.  Background poles were independently discovered at each
sampled positive Bloch wave number.  Local line segments were retained only
after a global one-to-one assignment passed three hard tests: identical
open/closed channel signature, groove-mode overlap exceeding 0.95, and
$|\Delta\Omega|<0.035$.  The accepted segments have minimum overlap 0.99875.
They terminate rather than being forced across a Rayleigh square-root cut;
adjacent-sheet continuations are omitted.  The target branch was continued
separately with consecutive-eigenvector overlap and has minimum stored overlap
0.99288 over $0\leq\kappa\leq0.5$.  Guided states are shown in grey and
excluded from the radiative-$Q$ color mapping.  The color scale spans
$0\leq\log_{10}Q\leq12$, with high-$Q$ states assigned the most vivid colors.
No interpolation or smoothing was used.

The target branch also reaches a symmetry-protected BIC at $\kappa=0$:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Omega_{\Gamma}&=0.883968664686884+2.27\times10^{-17}\ii,\\
 Q_{\Gamma}&=1.95\times10^{16}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:s-gamma-bic}
\end{equation}
At the same truncation as the Rayleigh endpoint,
$\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max}=9.12\times10^{-14}$,
$r_{\mathrm{raw}}=1.83\times10^{-12}$, and
$|A_0|/\|\bm C\|=1.61\times10^{-16}$.  The $n=\pm1$ components are nonzero but
evanescent.  Thus the $\Gamma$ state is a conventional symmetry BIC on the
same hybridized band, not a second Rayleigh BIC.

\section{Isolated-cavity and nonperiodic-dimer modes}

To expose the physical origin without invoking the periodic lattice, we solve
three auxiliary outgoing eigenproblems on an infinite rigid baffle: the large
groove alone, the small groove alone, and the two grooves at their final
separation.  These calculations retain the aperture radiation integral and
the full transverse cosine basis; no end correction is fitted.

The large groove supports an odd transverse ($q=1$-dominated) pole close to
the Rayleigh-BIC frequency.  The small groove supplies a much broader
$q=0$-dominated response.  Placing the grooves together hybridizes these
families and reduces their radiative linewidth, but the nonperiodic dimer is
still leaky.  The results are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:cavitymodes}.

onto the real Rayleigh line.

\section{Two-coordinate source reduction}

The mechanism plot in Fig.~3 of the Letter is a reduction of the full
modal-matching eigenvector, not an independently fitted $2\times2$ temporal
coupled-mode Hamiltonian.  We group the dominant aperture coefficients into
large- and small-groove coordinates.  For exterior order $n$, their
contributions to the velocity-continuity row are
\begin{equation}
 J_n=j_{nL}+j_{nS},\qquad
 j_{n\ell}=\ii\sum_{q\in\ell}D_{n,\ell q}
 (V_d)_{\ell q,\ell q}C_{\ell q}.
 \label{eq:s-source}
\end{equation}
Here $D_{n,\ell q}$ is the aperture-overlap matrix in the lower block of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:s-fulloperator}.  For a finite-flux order,
\begin{equation}
 A_n=-\frac{k_0}{k_{y,n}}J_n.
 \label{eq:s-source-to-pressure}
\end{equation}
Consequently $J_0=0$ is equivalent to $A_0=0$.  At the Rayleigh endpoint,
however, $k_{y,-1}=0$: $J_{-1}=0$ enforces the normal-velocity condition but
does not alone determine the grazing pressure.  The independent full-operator
calculation gives $A_{-1}=0$ as reported in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:s-strict} and \eqref{eq:s-fullres}.

The exact endpoint eigenvector gives
\begin{align}
 |j_{0L}|/|j_{0S}|&=1,&
 \arg(j_{0L}/j_{0S})&=\pi,\\
 |j_{-1,L}|/|j_{-1,S}|&=1,&
 \arg(j_{-1,L}/j_{-1,S})&=\pi.
 \label{eq:s-sourcebalance}
\end{align}
The normalized complex closure errors are $2.58\times10^{-16}$ for $n=0$
and $8.09\times10^{-17}$ for $n=-1$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:s-sourcebalance}
is the quantitative content of Fig.~3(c,d) in the Letter.

For one internal coordinate, both source rows can vanish only if its coupling
to each row vanishes separately.  With two coordinates, a relative complex
weight can cancel one row, while geometry and Bloch phase can align the
cancellation conditions of both rows.  This is a degree-of-freedom
interpretation of the present structure, not a universal theorem that every
Rayleigh BIC must contain two geometrically distinct cavities.

\section{Bounded single-groove comparison}

We also searched a declared family of one large rectangular groove per period,
retaining transverse modes.  At the exact $n=-1$ Rayleigh relation, the search
covered
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 0.04\leq\kappa\leq0.25,\qquad
 0.04\leq d/a\leq2.0,\\
 0.50\leq w/a\leq0.95.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
A deterministic 1800-point survey followed by 14 bounded local refinements
gave a best high-truncation residual
\begin{equation}
 (\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max})_{\mathrm{one\ groove}}=0.124588
\end{equation}
at $(N,K)=(221,44)$, compared with
$4.91\times10^{-16}$ for the two-groove state.  The best one-groove vector
had a transverse-mode fraction of 0.961, so its failure is not caused by a
single-mode cavity approximation.  This result shows the practical role of
the second cavity within the searched rectangular family; it is not a no-go
theorem for arbitrary compound or internally structured single resonators.

\section{Field localization and dimensional similarity}

The eigenfields in Fig.~2(e) were reconstructed from the homogeneous
coefficients on five periods and decomposed by Floquet order.  All states use
the same normalization.  At the Rayleigh BIC, the $n=0$ and $n=-1$ fields are
identically absent, and $n=+1$ decays exponentially.  Below the opening, only
$n=0$ is propagating; above it, both $n=0$ and $n=-1$ propagate.  This
comparison makes the channel topology visible without assigning power to an
evanescent order.

The ideal rigid-fluid equations are dimensionally scale invariant.  If all
lengths are multiplied by $s$ while the normalized geometry is fixed,
\begin{equation}
 f_{\BIC}(c,s)=\frac{\Omega_{\BIC}c}{sa_0}
 =180~\mathrm{kHz}\left(\frac{c}{1500~\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}\right)\frac{1}{s}.
 \label{eq:s-scaling}
\end{equation}
Thus, with frequency retuning, $c=1400$--$1600~\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ maps to
$f_{\BIC}=168$--$192~\mathrm{kHz}$ for the same dimensions.  This is an exact
similarity relation, not robustness of a fixed-frequency device: changing
$c$ while holding $f$, geometry, and angle fixed detunes both the Rayleigh
condition and the radiation zero.

\section{Scattering observables and experimental data gate}

The driven maps in Fig.~4 were evaluated with $(N,K)=(345,43)$ on 91 angles
and 57 adaptive detunings per angle.  The maximum lossless energy-closure
error was $5.21\times10^{-5}$.  The exact BIC row is left dark because a
strict homogeneous BIC cannot be excited by an ideal external plane wave.
The representative field in Fig.~4(e) is a nearby driven state at
$\theta=7.74434^{\circ}$ and $f=180.12125~\mathrm{kHz}$, for which the two
propagating efficiencies are $\eta_0=0.58713$ and
$\eta_{-1}=0.41287$.

The complex field at a scan height $y_s$ can be projected as
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 p_{\mathrm{sc}}(x,y_s)&=\sum_n\widetilde A_n(y_s)
 \e^{-\ii k_{x,n}x},\\
 A_n&=\widetilde A_n(y_s)\e^{+\ii k_{y,n}y_s}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:s-fieldfit}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:s-fieldfit} separates the directly measured grazing
pressure amplitude from its zero normal power.
